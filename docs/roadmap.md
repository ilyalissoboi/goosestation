# GooseStation roadmap

What to build next, in order of priority, with what each item waits on. Written on 2026-10-24,
after the memory card sync (#2), the rebrand (#4) and the new logo (#5); last updated on 2026-11-20,
after adding what players notice most from the upstream gaps (items 21 to 30).

**Size** is a rough effort guess: **S** a day or two, **M** a week or two, **L** longer.
**After** names the items that must be finished first. Items from DuckStation's post-fork feature
list are tagged with their priority there ([upstream-features.md](upstream-features.md)); they are
built clean-room, from behaviour and our own design, never from DuckStation's code after
`a5bf1ba5cee7`.

## 5. What players notice (from the upstream gaps)

The gaps in [upstream-features.md](upstream-features.md) that an ordinary player notices most while
playing ("What players notice most" there), as far as they can be built now. Its first two, accuracy
fixes and per-game fixes in the game database, stay as items 19 and 18: they need games tested or
reported one by one, which GooseStation doesn't have yet. Each is built clean-room, from behaviour and
our own design.

| # | Item | Size | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| 21 | GPU work on its own thread, in two stages (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-20-gpu-thread-and-pacing-design.md`). Stage 1 is done: every renderer's device and its work on a GPU thread of its own, desktop (Qt and Big Picture) and Android, behind "Use a GPU thread" (`[GPU] UseGPUThread`, on by default for every renderer), at most a frame ahead of the emulation thread; the hardware renderer split into a front end on the emulation thread (timing, PGXP, software readbacks) and a back end on the GPU thread, commands handed over in batches; the counters follow (the GPU thread's CPU use and input latency in the per-second line, GPU % only when it's plausible). Nothing is drawn differently (every regtest frame identical, Mac and Windows, thread on and off). Measured interleaved with the build before it, at unlimited speed and 1x: faster with the thread on every renderer, D3D11 1–2%, D3D12 2%, Vulkan 3–5% and OpenGL 13–20% on Windows, Metal 11–21%, MoltenVK 6–11% and OpenGL 35–39% on the Mac, and on the AYN Thor fast-forward OpenGL ES 11–16% and Vulkan 2–4%, short of the 20% aimed for: at fast-forward the emulation thread's own work dominates (the SPU alone takes a fifth of it), and the GPU work the thread takes away is about 5% of it on Vulkan. The heavy OpenGL ES case on the Thor (Vagrant Story at 8x, 4x MSAA, CRT-Lottes) drops below 59.8 VPS in fewer seconds than before (0–1 a run against 0–4), and input lag is a little lower with the thread than without. With the thread off, unlimited speed is 2–6% slower than before (the commands are still built). Stage 2, pacing on the thread, is item 22. | L | none |
| 22 | Frame presentation and pacing, in stages (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-20-gpu-thread-and-pacing-design.md`, "Stage 2, revised"). Measured on the AYN Thor's own screen, frames were already even (the swap chain absorbs the emulation thread's uneven timing); what was left was lag, a frame reaching the screen 36–37 ms after the GPU finished it. Stage 2a, Android, is done: with sync to host refresh rate (now on by default there, turned on once in older settings), frames lock to the screen's refreshes from Choreographer's timeline, each presented as soon as it's done and started so it lands just before the compositor takes it; where that is, each device's own, is learnt from every present's feedback (`VK_GOOGLE_display_timing`, `EGL_ANDROID_get_frame_timestamps`); the console's frame rate is asked of the surface; vsync is off while fast-forwarding. On the Thor at 60 Hz, input lag is 7.7–10.2 ms lower (36–38 ms to 27–29 ms at 1×, 25 to 21 ms in the heavy OpenGL ES case) with 96–98% of seconds even; at 120 Hz the game runs at 60.00 frames a second with 96% of frames on two refreshes, but only 67% of seconds even, which needs frames tagged with Choreographer's frame timelines (`ASurfaceControl`), a further stage. Stage 2b, desktop present waits, is done: vsync is three-way (`[Display] VSyncMode`: Automatic, On, Off; an old `VSync` key moves to it); with On, each present is waited on until it's shown (Vulkan `VK_KHR_present_id` and `present_wait`, Metal's presented handlers; D3D12 and D3D11 not yet: on the Windows machine's screen, DXGI's frame-latency waitable didn't reliably mark a present leaving the queue, nor its frame statistics a present shown, so D3D is as before), for the frame just presented or, while frames keep missing, the one before, and the waits' times feed 2a's screen lock; Metal also locks without vsync, from its presented times, where the screen has a fixed refresh (the Mac's ProMotion screen doesn't: nothing changes there); sync to host is on by default on desktop too (turned on once in older settings) and changes the game's speed only with vsync on. Measured on the Mac, display sync costs about a refresh over unsynced (and, windowed, tear-free) presents whatever the waits do, so Automatic leaves vsync off on every renderer for now: at the defaults nothing changes (Metal 1× input to screen 2.9–3.1 ms against 2.8–4.3, the same speed on Metal, MoltenVK and OpenGL; every regtest frame identical on the Mac and Windows). On the Windows machine's screen, D3D12 and D3D11 at the defaults and at vsync On match the build before (D3D12 at On: 84.6 ms from input to screen, both). D3D's present waits, and turning Automatic on per API once measured, are next. | M | 21 helps |
| 23 | Shader and pipeline compilation on several threads, so a game's first scenes stutter less while its pipelines are built. | M | none |
| 24 | The audio stream reworked for slow devices: fading around underruns, more buffering when emulation can't keep up, and stretching on the audio thread. Fewer crackles and dropouts, on the Thor especially. | M | none |

| 26 | PGXP improvements: PGXP for lines and unaligned loads and stores, a more precise CPU mode, and a reworked depth buffer, for less wobbling geometry and z-fighting when upscaled. Checked with our own test games and regression tests. | M | none |
| 27 | Upscaling defaults: a rewritten automatic internal resolution, nearest sprite filtering by default, and an option not to upscale direct-colour textures, for fewer seams and artefacts in 2D at higher resolutions. | S | none |
| 28 | Patch codes with options: patches that offer choices (a widescreen ratio, a frame rate), shown as settings in the patch lists on Qt, Big Picture and Android. The system only: the widescreen and 60 fps patches themselves come from our own testing and public sources, as they're found, like the game database. | M | none |

## Later

- Register GooseStation with RetroAchievements (item 6, low priority for now); until then its user agent
  may be treated as an unknown emulator and hardcore mode refused.
- Importing settings, memory cards and save states from an existing DuckStation install.
- Multitap memory cards in the UI (upstream P1, left out of item 11).
- A Discord application for GooseStation, so rich presence can be turned back on.
- An on-screen keyboard of our own in Big Picture (upstream P1). Every platform already has a way
  to type (item 14), so it would only help a desktop driven by a controller alone, outside Steam,
  such as a TV PC.
- Flathub publishing: needs screenshots, media mirroring and the `flatpak-builder-lint repo` check
  restored.
- The P2 items in [upstream-features.md](upstream-features.md): texture filters, display scaling,
  bezels, save state and game list improvements, cheat management, more controllers, achievement UI
  and the rest.

## Suggested order

1 → 2 → 4 → 5, with 7 and 9 alongside: a first release with sync tested for real. Then
14 and 12 (in that order: 14 gives Big Picture the platforms' keyboards, which 12's settings
use). With 12, 14 and 15 done, Android (16) is next. Items 17 and 18 block nothing and can be
picked up at any time.

Items 21 to 30 are ordered by what players notice: 21 and 22 together (smoothness), then 24 and 25
(sound and loading, both small), then 23, then the enhancements 26 to 30.
